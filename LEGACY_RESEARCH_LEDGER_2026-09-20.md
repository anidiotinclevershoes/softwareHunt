# Software Opportunity Hunt — Agent Operating Manual & Research Ledger

**Status:** 20 September 2026  
**Purpose:** A standalone handoff for an agent continuing the software-opportunity hunt.  
**Primary objective:** Find a *very small* number of unusually strong standalone software/SaaS opportunities genuinely worth practitioner validation. Zero survivors is preferable to promoting mediocre ideas.

---

## 1. Mission

This is **not an idea-generation exercise**.

The job is to discover consequential work that is poorly owned by existing systems, organisations and AI, and then aggressively try to disprove that a viable standalone software business exists.

The hunt should prefer evidence over novelty and should treat attractive whitespace with suspicion.

A useful high-level question is:

> **What consequential truth is difficult for existing systems, organisations or AI to own reliably?**

“Consequential” is deliberately broad. It may concern:

- money;
- contractual entitlement;
- rights;
- evidence;
- ownership;
- scarce assets;
- operational decisions;
- reputation;
- creative output;
- significant time;
- significant emotional value.

Do **not** reduce the hunt to financial reconciliation, regulation, compliance, or “replace the spreadsheet.”

---

## 2. Geographic and Commercial Constraints

Default geographic priority:

1. Ireland
2. United Kingdom
3. EU / EEA

US evidence can reveal useful market structures, terminology and mature-workflow analogues, but a US-only opportunity is not a final candidate.

A practical acquisition test applies throughout:

> Could the founder realistically identify and get the first 10 prospects onto calls from Ireland/UK without already being an industry insider?

The business does not need a huge TAM. A narrow, high-value vertical can be attractive.

For example, a business with 20 customers paying €2,000/month may be preferable to one requiring 20,000 consumers paying €5/month.

Consumer, prosumer, creative and cultural opportunities remain valid. Do not automatically favour B2B compliance products merely because they are easier to find through search.

---

## 3. Promotion Bar

A strong opportunity should combine most of the following:

- repeated meaningful pain or desire;
- measurable economic or emotional consequence;
- weak current solution;
- weak enough direct competition after aggressive searching;
- clear and small initial product;
- obvious first-use payoff;
- credible willingness to pay;
- accessible first customers;
- repeat use, or an honestly valuable transactional model;
- useful accumulated data/history;
- defensibility beyond an LLM prompt;
- manageable technical and integration burden;
- manageable liability/compliance burden;
- credible Ireland/UK beachhead;
- plausible wider UK/EU potential;
- ability to become commercially worthwhile without enormous scale.

The threshold for promotion is intentionally high.

# 4. Core Research Philosophy

## 4.1 Follow consequences, not software complaints

Weak starting point:

> “People in Industry X still use Excel.”

Better starting point:

> “£X can be lost because nobody reliably knows whether Y is currently true.”

Search first for the consequence:

- revenue leakage;
- underbilling;
- short payment;
- incorrect settlement;
- unclaimed entitlement;
- missed credits;
- missed recoveries;
- overpayments;
- disputed deductions;
- forgotten contractual rights;
- unbilled extras;
- unexplained variance;
- aged unmatched balances;
- lost evidence;
- ownership ambiguity;
- rights that survive long after a transaction;
- costly decisions made without authoritative current truth.

Then determine **what truth would have prevented the consequence**.

Only after that should software be considered.

---

## 4.2 Prefer boundaries over deficiencies

One of the strongest recurring discoveries is:

> **Prefer problems created by boundaries, not deficiencies.**

A deficiency is:

- poor UI;
- missing ERP feature;
- ugly spreadsheet;
- inconvenient workflow.

A boundary exists where two legitimate sources of reality can disagree:

- supplier ↔ buyer;
- claimant ↔ acquiring authority;
- contract ↔ transaction;
- operational system ↔ finance;
- landlord ↔ tenant;
- licensor ↔ licensee;
- owner ↔ custodian;
- evidence ↔ negotiated current position;
- technical acceptance ↔ commercial payment;
- seller ↔ buyer after completion.

These boundaries are attractive because neither existing party/system can necessarily be trusted as the sole authority.

---

## 4.3 Search for “shadow truth”

A particularly useful model is **system-to-system / party-to-party shadow truth**.

Example:

```text
Contract says what should happen
+
Operational system says what happened
+
Counterparty says what it owes
+
Bank/ledger says what was paid
=
Independent truth that nobody currently owns
```

The software opportunity, if one exists, is often that missing independent truth layer.

# 5. Discovery Methods

## 5.1 Money/value-first search

Start with evidence that value disappears or becomes uncertain.

Useful search concepts include:

```text
underpayment
unclaimed
missed credit
recovery
short payment
incorrect settlement
unbilled
forgotten retention
contract leakage
revenue leakage
margin leakage
true-up
unreconciled
unmatched
disputed deduction
payment entitlement
contingent payment
retrospective rebate
commission entitlement
claim rejected
write-off
```

Do not merely search these terms generically. Pair them with industries, contractual relationships, job titles, professional services and transaction types.

---

## 5.2 Paid human as prototype

Job archaeology remains useful, but only when tightened.

Look for jobs where the recurring work resembles:

```text
extract
→ reconcile
→ classify
→ calculate
→ investigate exceptions
→ produce recurring output
```

High-signal examples are humans whose job exists because **two parties or systems disagree about consequential reality**.

Weak evidence:

- general administrators;
- relationship managers;
- negotiators;
- roles dominated by judgement;
- physical operations.

Strong evidence:

- claims/recovery analysts;
- reconciliation analysts;
- warranty controllers;
- commission analysts;
- commercial auditors;
- entitlement specialists;
- people maintaining recurring contract-to-payment workpapers.

A salary is evidence that the work has value. It is **not** evidence that software can replace the role.

---

## 5.3 Consultant-deliverable inversion

Search for consultants repeatedly selling structured outputs such as:

- reconciliation;
- recovery audit;
- entitlement calculation;
- cost verification;
- claim workpaper;
- valuation schedule;
- exception report;
- commercial audit;
- certification pack.

Ask:

> How much of the fee is deterministic data transformation and how much is actual expertise/judgement/liability?

An attractive shape may allow software to automate the deterministic preparation while retaining the professional for the judgement kernel.

Do not propose replacing regulated expertise merely because a consultant is expensive.

---

## 5.4 Mature-workflow counterfactual

When apparent whitespace appears, investigate a more mature version of the same workflow:

- another European country;
- a mature US market;
- enterprise software;
- an older regulatory regime;
- an adjacent mature industry.

Possible results:

1. **Specialist SaaS already exists** → strong competitor warning.
2. **ERP absorbed the job** → weak standalone opportunity.
3. **Consultants persist** → investigate consultant inversion.
4. **Manual workflow persists despite substantial value** → interesting.
5. **Process is actually trivial** → kill.

This became especially important after the DRS investigation.

---

## 5.5 Ugly data + durable truth

Prefer opportunities where continued use creates proprietary knowledge of reality:

- contract interpretation history;
- entitlement history;
- negotiated matter state;
- asset history;
- transaction classifications;
- accepted/rejected claim history;
- evidence provenance;
- supplier/customer mappings;
- exception-resolution history.

A good product should often be more useful after six months because it remembers truth that a fresh LLM session or competitor does not possess.

---

## 5.6 Random-word / odd-pair discovery

Random words are retained, but **only as transport into unexpected economic behaviour**.

They are not brainstorming prompts.

The method:

1. Choose unrelated words.
2. Derive conceptual properties from them.
3. Pair those properties with commercial behaviour.
4. Search the resulting economic/contractual shapes.
5. Follow evidence rather than the original word.

Example seed set used:

- Kipper
- Blob
- Fight
- Mash
- Snub

Later conceptual seeds included:

- Hinge
- Crumb
- Echo
- Bruise
- Ghost

These generated useful structures:

### Echo
A transaction ended, but an economic right remains alive.

This led toward:

- overage/clawback;
- post-termination commission;
- residual royalty rights;
- historical wayleave payments.

### Ghost
Something economically real exists outside the system that is expected to know about it.

This led toward:

- customer-owned tooling;
- consignment stock;
- returnable packaging;
- off-site construction materials;
- unrecorded contractual variations.

### Bruise
Value is disputed because an asset has been altered/damaged and parties disagree about responsibility.

This led toward:

- rental damage;
- fleet end-of-lease damage;
- 3PL goods damage;
- art transit condition;
- utility works damage.

### Hinge
A small factual event changes ownership, payment or liability.

This led toward:

- FAT/SAT acceptance;
- title transfer;
- vesting;
- milestone payments;
- off-hire/on-hire timestamps.

### Crumb
Small amounts/residual value are individually ignorable but aggregate materially.

This led toward:

- forgotten retentions;
- supplier credits;
- residual client balances;
- scrap proceeds;
- small freight claims.

The method is useful precisely because it gets the hunt away from familiar SaaS vocabulary.

---

# 6. Search-Batch Operating Rule

For broad continuation runs, the standing target is:

> **Attempt 100 materially distinct seeds/search branches before returning.**

Do not return early merely because an attractive anomaly appears.

The purpose is to avoid anchoring on the first interesting result.

Where practical, a deep run should also seek:

- many genuinely different semantic branches;
- at least half outside the neighbourhood of current candidates;
- multiple explicit competitor attacks;
- limited overconcentration on regulation/compliance/reconciliation.

If a technical/tool limitation prevents 100 attempts, report the actual count honestly.

Never invent:

- search counts;
- source counts;
- elapsed research time;
- autonomous/background research;
- market-size numbers.

---

# 7. The Competitor Kill

Every serious anomaly must be attacked.

Search:

1. exact proposition;
2. underlying job;
3. alternative terminology;
4. software/app/platform/SaaS;
5. recent entrants (especially 2025–26);
6. practitioner communities;
7. GitHub/open source/free tools where relevant;
8. incumbent functionality;
9. adjacent products;
10. manual substitutes;
11. mature-country analogue;
12. AI + spreadsheet substitution.

Read what products **actually do**. Landing-page category labels are not enough.

Recent research repeatedly found that an independently derived “new” proposition was already the exact landing-page copy of a 2025–26 startup.

---

# 8. Second Kill

Anything approaching candidate status receives a separate bear-case attack.

Try to establish:

- willingness to pay is weak;
- market is tiny;
- buyers are hard to reach;
- integrations dominate implementation;
- hidden/proprietary data is required;
- services dominate software;
- liability is unacceptable;
- frequency is too low;
- retention is weak;
- workflow requires major behaviour change;
- incumbent can trivially add the feature;
- AI + CSV provides enough value;
- apparent whitespace exists because customers simply do not care.

Finding an opportunity is not the stopping condition.

Failing to kill it is.

---

# 9. AI Substitution Test

Before promotion ask:

> Could the customer export a CSV/document set, upload it to ChatGPT/Copilot, explain the rules and obtain 80% of the value?

If yes, standalone software needs a strong reason to exist.

Valid reasons include:

- continuous integrations;
- durable mappings;
- multi-period state;
- immutable evidence;
- corrections across periods;
- approvals;
- workflow coordination;
- transaction scale;
- deterministic controls;
- proprietary outcome history.

“Better UX” is not sufficient.

---

# 10. Moat-Narrowing Kill Rule

This rule is critical.

One evidence-led refinement is legitimate.

Repeated refinement merely to escape competitors is not.

If reasoning becomes:

> “Competitor X does this, but ours is specifically for this slightly smaller customer / unusual case / narrower workflow…”

repeatedly, **kill the proposition**.

The DRS investigation demonstrated why.

---

# 11. The Missing-Category Warning

A major lesson:

> **Absence of competition is not automatically positive evidence.**

If:

- the industry is mature;
- the workflow is mature;
- meaningful software already surrounds it;
- consultants understand it;
- extensive search finds no specialist category;

ask:

> Why has nobody built this?

Possible answers:

- Excel is adequate;
- AI now makes Excel adequate;
- ERP configuration handles it;
- consultants efficiently absorb exceptions;
- frequency is too low;
- consequence is too small;
- workflow heterogeneity prevents productisation;
- buyer population is tiny;
- incumbent systems already remove most pain.

This lesson was learned most clearly from DRS producer reconciliation.

# 12. Retired / Downgraded Discovery Patterns

## Embarrassing spreadsheet hunt — retired as primary discovery

Earlier search focused heavily on:

> “We still use Excel because…”

This proved too noisy.

The 2025–26 micro-SaaS market is saturated with products whose entire pitch is replacing spreadsheets/email/WhatsApp in a vertical.

Spreadsheet use is now **secondary evidence only**.

Never promote an idea because:

> “They use Excel!”

Instead ask:

> What consequential truth is Excel currently carrying, and why can neither incumbent systems nor AI own it properly?

---

## “Why can't X do this?” searches — heavily downgraded

Complaints about Sage/Xero/ERP/etc. usually identify a missing feature, not a standalone company.

---

## Regulator-change radar — heavily downgraded

New obligations generate searchable pain but also attract SaaS builders immediately.

Use only where regulation creates a deeper independent-truth problem.

---

## Templates/forms archaeology — downgraded

Forms show workflow existence, not willingness to pay.

---

## Generic tribal-knowledge risk — downgraded

“Only Jane knows how this works” is insufficient unless loss from Jane being wrong/unavailable is measurable and significant.

---

# 13. Major Market Lesson: 2025–26 Micro-SaaS Saturation

Repeated searches produced a strong general finding:

> “Industry X still uses spreadsheets/email/WhatsApp” is no longer a useful discovery thesis by itself.

Many tiny verticals now have recent entrants with:

- polished landing pages;
- inexpensive SaaS;
- AI features;
- direct integrations;
- vertical-specific terminology.

Therefore the hunt moved from:

```text
manual workflow
→ build SaaS
```

to:

```text
consequential disagreement
→ missing authoritative truth
→ economic/other consequence
→ structural reason incumbents cannot own truth
→ structural reason AI alone is insufficient
→ evidence humans are paid to resolve it
→ competitor attack
→ mature-market test
→ second kill
```


- promoter-side systems;

## B. Creative-industry expenditure-credit workpaper

**Status:** KILLED after dedicated adversarial validation on 20 September 2026.

The proposed UK workflow was:

```text
production ledger
→ production attribution
→ qualifying / non-qualifying expenditure
→ core / non-core where relevant
→ UK / non-UK
→ connected parties
→ evidence/provenance
→ AVEC/VGEC computation/workpaper
→ accountant-ready handoff
```

The underlying work is real, but the standalone SaaS proposition failed the second attack:

- HMRC/BFI already provide AVEC/VGEC spreadsheet stencils/templates for the deterministic claim-preparation layer;
- specialist advisers provide enhanced free spreadsheets covering multiple productions/accounting periods, categorisation, apportionment and HMRC-ready schedules;
- established Corporation Tax software supports the AVEC/VGEC filing/data-entry layer;
- specialist creative-industry accountants and tax advisers retain the judgement-heavy work;
- AI + production ledger + regulator template + accountant can plausibly substitute for much of the proposed lightweight workpaper;
- the difficult residual questions carry professional judgement and tax liability.

The market is economically meaningful, but the product is squeezed between:

```text
simple claim
→ HMRC/BFI/free specialist spreadsheet

complex/high-value claim
→ production accountant + specialist adviser + tax software
```

**Kill reason:** the missing independent software layer does not appear valuable enough to own. The deterministic portion is already cheaply templated; the valuable residual work is professional judgement.

**Standing lesson:** when the regulator already supplies a structured spreadsheet implementing the deterministic core of a workflow, treat that as strong negative evidence unless a separate consequential truth exists that the spreadsheet cannot own.

# 16. Banked Anomaly — Landowner Overage / Clawback Monitoring

**Status:** BANKED after a 70-search adversarial attack on 20 September 2026.  
**Do not promote or keep narrowing without genuinely new evidence.**

The structural problem:

A landowner sells property but retains contractual rights to future money if a specified event occurs, potentially years or decades later.

Possible triggers:

- planning permission;
- implementation of planning;
- development;
- onward sale;
- increase in development value;
- unit sales;
- other bespoke events.

Potential truth chain:

```text
historic agreement
→ bespoke trigger definition
→ real-world external event
→ calculation
→ notification requirement
→ evidence
→ payment
→ remaining future rights
```

Why it is interesting:

- the person owed money may not control the triggering event;
- they may not naturally observe it;
- rights can outlive the people who negotiated them;
- agreements can survive ownership changes;
- land agents can be retained specifically to monitor trigger events and payments;
- planning/property data creates potentially machine-observable external events.

The attack confirmed that the underlying problem is real: sophisticated sellers can retain land agents for the life of clawback/overage clauses; rights can remain active for decades; public-sector guidance recognises recurring portfolio monitoring/governance; and real disputes can involve substantial sums.

However, the commercial shape did not clear the bar. The market appears split between individual landowners with one/few low-frequency agreements, professional land agents/solicitors who bundle monitoring with interpretation/valuation/enforcement, and large institutions with internal teams, procurement complexity and broader systems.

Raw planning/property event monitoring is also becoming cheap. To remain differentiated, the proposition repeatedly narrows toward bespoke agreement ingestion, contractual trigger interpretation, external-event correlation, entitlement calculation and evidence/right-state maintenance. That increasingly enters legal interpretation, valuation and professional judgement.

**Bank reason:** strong structural problem, but insufficient evidence of a dense, reachable repeat buyer population for standalone SaaS, combined with cheap event-monitoring substitutes and increasing professional/liability burden as the proposition narrows.

Retain the broader discovery pattern:

> Dormant right + externally observable event + beneficiary is not naturally present when the event occurs.

Do **not** confuse this with the killed general DRS proposition.

Possible narrow Irish issue:

```text

− Irish deposits recovered from ROI customers
− legitimate export reimbursement
=
potential unrecovered deposit cash
```

Potential users: Irish beverage importers/distributors buying international-barcode stock and exporting some stock outside ROI.

- annual € value;
- claim mechanics;
- evidence requirements;
- missed/rejected claims;
- existing recovery process;
- ERP/accountant/Re-turn automation;
- competitors;
- Irish TAM;

Kill immediately if refunds are trivial/automatic, population tiny, or missed money lacks evidence.

## DRS producer reconciliation — KILLED

This is the most important kill because it changed the methodology.

Original attractive hypothesis:

```text
producer ERP truth
vs
Re-turn DRS truth
→ independent reconciliation
```

Deep validation showed:

- Re-turn generally invoices from producer-reported POM data rather than an independent transaction ledger;
- the attractive “ERP vs Re-turn financial truth” boundary weakened;
- remaining work was largely POM preparation/assurance;
- Re-turn manual reporting reduced small-producer friction;
- sophisticated producers can use ERP/implementation support;
- Ecosurety materially reduced whitespace;
- mature European DRS markets did not validate a standalone lightweight reconciliation category;
- extensive searching failed to uncover the expected ugly recurring reconciliation workbooks;
- Excel/Power Query/AI substitution was substantial;
- proposition repeatedly narrowed toward complex/cross-border/importer/international-barcode exceptions.

The **moat-narrowing kill rule** fired.

Do not resurrect general DRS producer reconciliation because no exact lightweight competitor appears in a future search.

# 21. Search-Pattern Lessons

## Lesson 1 — Spreadsheet pain is cheap evidence

Almost every industry has spreadsheets.

That does not imply a company should exist to replace them.

---

## Lesson 2 — Paid humans are stronger evidence, but still not enough

Agricultural warranty controllers looked superb until incumbent DMS products were examined.

Human labour proves value exists.

It does not prove software whitespace exists.

---

## Lesson 3 — Search the boundary from both sides

If investigating money owed by A to B, search:

- how A calculates it;
- how B checks it;
- accountants/auditors;
- disputes/litigation;
- job descriptions;
- contract clauses;
- software used by each party.

Often one side has software that is invisible when searching from the other side.

---

## Lesson 4 — Litigation can reveal hidden economic machinery

Court cases and disputes can expose:

- exact payment formulas;
- contingent rights;
- record-access problems;
- years-long evidence histories;
- amounts large enough to justify litigation.

This proved useful in searches around contingent consideration and contractual triggers.

Litigation is discovery evidence, not automatic product evidence.

---

## Lesson 5 — “Echo rights” are promising

A useful new pattern from random-word hunting:

> The original transaction has ended, but a right remains economically alive.

Examples:

- overage/clawback;
- trailing commissions;
- royalties;
- contingent consideration;
- residual property rights.

These are attractive because ordinary operational systems often stop caring once the transaction closes.

The next hunt should continue probing this pattern beyond obvious property/IP categories.

---

## Lesson 6 — External-event triggers are promising

A particularly interesting combination is:

```text
private contract
+
public/external event
→ money becomes payable
```

Overage is the clearest current example.

Potential external events might include:

- planning decisions;
- registrations;
- ownership changes;
- company events;
- public tenders/awards;
- certifications;
- asset commissioning;
- market prices;
- court/regulatory decisions.

The opportunity becomes stronger where the beneficiary does **not control the event** and therefore risks never noticing it.

---

## Lesson 7 — Hinge events can carry disproportionate value

Small factual events can alter:

- ownership;
- liability;
- payment;
- warranty;
- risk transfer.

FAT/SAT acceptance is one example.

Search for situations where a seemingly administrative sign-off changes a large commercial position.

---

## Lesson 8 — Counterparty self-reporting is fertile, but mature

Many attractive problems have:

```text
Party B reports activity
→ Party A is paid based on B's report
```

Examples:

- royalties;
- extraction;
- commissions;
- turnover rent;
- rebates.

This is structurally strong but commercially mature. Expect audit/software incumbents.

Look for unusual forms of self-reported value where mature assurance tooling has not already emerged.

---

## Lesson 9 — “The most expensive claim is the one never filed”

Recovery workflows often focus on rejected claims.

A more interesting failure can occur earlier:

> nobody realises an eligible event occurred.

This distinction matters.

Potential product:

```text
detect entitlement
→ prove entitlement
→ submit
→ reconcile
```

rather than merely:

```text
manage submitted claims
```

But existing platforms frequently expand upstream, so competitor attack remains essential.

---

## Lesson 10 — Do not confuse elegant structure with market evidence

Toll manufacturing has a beautiful ownership/yield boundary.

That does not mean customers will buy a standalone product.

The hunt must repeatedly distinguish:

**“This is intellectually neat”**

from:

**“People lose enough value often enough to buy software.”**

---

# 22. Recommended Search Shapes Going Forward

Do not merely repeat old keywords.

Explore structures such as:

### Dormant contractual rights

```text
old agreement
+ later external event
→ new entitlement
```

### Hidden trigger

```text
event occurs outside beneficiary's systems
→ beneficiary should act/get paid
```

### Custody without ownership

```text
A owns asset/material
B physically controls it
→ quantity/condition/value disagreement
```

### Technical event → commercial consequence

```text
inspection/test/signoff
→ payment/title/warranty/liability changes
```

### Self-reported economic base

```text
counterparty reports sales/usage/output
× bespoke contract
→ payment owed
```

### Long-lived negotiated truth

```text
years of documents
→ current agreed/disputed state
→ consequential decision/payment
```

### Rights surviving personnel/system turnover

```text
contract survives
people leave
systems change
event later occurs
→ nobody remembers entitlement
```

### Deterministic professional preparation

```text
messy source data
→ rules/calculation/classification
→ structured workpaper
→ professional judgement
```

The last category is where the creative-industry expenditure-credit workpaper originated.

# 23. Research Discipline

For every anomaly record:

```text
1. Who loses / benefits?
2. What is the consequence?
3. What exact truth is missing?
4. Which parties/systems each possess part of that truth?
5. Why can't an incumbent own it?
6. Why can't AI + files solve enough of it?
7. Who currently performs the work?
8. What do they get paid?
9. How frequently does it recur?
10. What evidence exists of actual loss?
11. What software already touches it?
12. What is the mature-market analogue?
13. What would the smallest viable product actually do?
14. How would the first 10 customers be reached?
15. What is the strongest reason nobody has built it?
16. What would immediately kill it?
```

Do not fill unknowns with assumptions.

Mark them unknown and search them.

# 24. Status Vocabulary

Use disciplined status labels.

## KILLED
Enough evidence exists that the proposition should not consume further discovery effort absent genuinely new information.

## BANKED ANOMALY
Interesting structural evidence exists, but not enough to justify deep validation.

## VALIDATION TRACK
Survived enough desk research to justify a dedicated adversarial investigation/practitioner validation.

## PRACTITIONER VALIDATION
Desk research has reached diminishing returns. Real practitioners should now be asked questions designed to kill the hypothesis.

Avoid “candidate” language unless the evidence genuinely warrants it.

Never jump from search result to “build.”

# 28. Final Operating Principle

The hunt has progressively moved away from:

> Find industries with bad software.

toward:

> Find consequential reality that falls between systems, parties or time periods — then determine whether a small independent system can become the trusted owner of that reality.

That is the current research thesis.

The strongest opportunities found so far are not simply workflows that are annoying.

They are situations where:

- **something valuable is genuinely true;**
- **no existing participant can reliably own the complete truth;**
- **being wrong or failing to notice matters;**
- **the truth changes over time;**
- **history/evidence makes the software progressively more valuable;**
- **and neither a generic incumbent nor an ad-hoc LLM session is an adequate substitute.**

Keep searching for that.
