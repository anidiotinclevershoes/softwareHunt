# Software Opportunity Hunt — V3.5 Follow-the-Money / Convenience Amendment

**Approved:** 2026-10-13 by explicit user instruction  
**Status:** active authority; supplements V3/V3.1/V3.2/V3.3/V3.4 without weakening commercial, complete-result, reliability, founder-operation or BUILD-READY gates  
**Scope:** money-first discovery, premium convenience spending, spend evidence, and software-replaceable delegation

## Purpose

The hunt has repeatedly found documented workflows and real inconvenience without finding enough purchase energy. V3.5 changes the discovery direction:

> First find individuals or companies demonstrably willing to spend meaningful money for speed, delegation, certainty or freedom from hassle. Then determine what convenience they are buying and whether software can deliver the complete useful result.

Do not begin with an annoying workflow and speculate that somebody might pay. Begin with an observed payment, fee, premium tier, contract, managed service, outsourced role, rush charge or repeated purchase. Trace the money to the actual payer and purchase trigger before proposing software.

This amendment does not assume wealthy people or companies will buy any convenient product. It does not convert expensive professional services into software demand, relax competition, permit partial-feature products, or require a survivor.

## 1. Money-first discovery sequence

For every counted attempt, follow this order:

1. **Observed spend:** identify a real paid transaction, current public fee, premium tier, procurement award, outsourced service, concierge package, expedite charge, agency retainer, assistant/coordinator role or other credible evidence that money changes hands.
2. **Payer and trigger:** identify who pays, who approves, what event causes the purchase and why it is worth paying now.
3. **Convenience purchased:** isolate the specific burden being removed—time, learning, searching, coordination, chasing, scheduling, form completion, data assembly, comparison, transformation, submission, waiting, error risk or availability.
4. **Premium decomposition:** separate convenience from expertise, judgement, trust, liability transfer, relationship access, physical work, capital, licensing and status. Record which component commands the money.
5. **Complete software result:** ask whether a first version can take accessible inputs and complete the result the payer currently buys, with an objective acceptance test and a practical return path.
6. **Commercial attack:** test recurrence/volume, buyer access, acquisition, alternatives, switching, support, AI substitution, incumbent response and the strongest explanation that the spend will not move to software.

If the spend cannot be established after two targeted searches, mark the attempt incomplete and move on. Do not reverse-engineer imaginary willingness to pay from inconvenience.

## 2. Spend-evidence grades

Record the strongest observed level:

| Grade | Evidence |
|---|---|
| M0 | No credible payment or budget evidence |
| M1 | Vendor claim, estimated staff time, unsourced price, or hypothetical value only |
| M2 | Live public price, fee schedule, paid listing, salary/job budget, or current purchasable offer |
| M3 | Independent reviews, named customer evidence, multiple established sellers, repeat marketplace transactions, or buyer-side description of paying |
| M4 | Buyer-side invoice, contract, procurement award, reported budget/spend, direct payment, deposit, paid pilot or equivalent primary evidence |

M2 is enough to count a real money-first direction. It is not proof that software can capture the spend. Prefer M3–M4 for promotion; multiple converging M2 sources may support a bounded outreach hypothesis when every other V3.3 gate passes. Never label a professional fee as software willingness to pay.

## 3. What counts as convenience

Convenience is a purchase whose material value comes from removing effort, elapsed time, coordination, learning, uncertainty or availability constraints while still producing a complete wanted outcome.

Useful convenience mechanisms include:

- done-for-you completion;
- faster or priority turnaround;
- searching, comparing and selecting;
- assembling scattered inputs;
- booking, scheduling and rescheduling;
- coordinating multiple parties;
- chasing and exception resolution;
- transforming information into an accepted output;
- submitting, filing or handing off;
- monitoring until completion;
- out-of-hours or on-demand availability;
- repeat execution without relearning the process.

A product must deliver the complete bounded convenience outcome. A checklist, dashboard, reminder, draft, recommendation or prettier interface is not enough when the buyer still performs the consequential work.

## 4. Convenience-premium decomposition

For each serious case, state why the customer pays the existing premium:

| Component | Can software ordinarily replace it? |
|---|---|
| clerical effort, information assembly, deterministic transformation | potentially |
| coordination, status chasing, scheduling and handoff | potentially, if integrations/support stay bounded |
| speed from automation | potentially |
| searchable memory, repeat execution and audit trail | potentially |
| specialist judgement or bespoke interpretation | only when V3.4 documentation and safe verification encode it |
| licensed approval, legal accountability or liability transfer | normally no |
| physical labour, inspection, logistics or equipment | no, unless the software result is independently valuable |
| personal trust, relationships, negotiation or privileged access | normally no |
| luxury signalling, taste or emotional reassurance | not assumed software-replaceable |

Kill when the money attaches mainly to a non-software component. Do not preserve a case by shrinking it to an incidental clerical feature.

## 5. Next explicit 100-attempt composition

For attempts **761–860**, this V3.5 mix replaces only the earlier default discovery composition:

- **40 company-paid delegation cases:** recurring managed services, administrative outsourcing, agency work, coordination, expediting or done-for-you completion bought primarily to save staff or executive time.
- **30 individual/prosumer premium-convenience cases:** time-poor or high-consideration buyers paying meaningful amounts to avoid learning, searching, coordination, delay or repeated effort.
- **30 explicit premium-differential cases:** rush, priority, concierge, white-glove or done-for-you tiers where the price difference from the standard route can be observed.

Every row must represent a distinct payer + trigger + purchased outcome, not a new noun for the same mechanism. At least 60 cases should be outside regulation, compliance, accounting reconciliation and standards-file conversion. Use odd/random seeds only to reach unfamiliar spending behaviour.

The V3.2 sidecar tests still apply after a money trail is found: accessible inputs, complete outcome, return path, observable spend, advantage exceeding adoption friction, recurrence and a structural reason for separate purchase. Competitor, validator, AI, access and second-kill searches are follow-ups and never additional attempts.

## 6. Evidence bands for “spend a lot”

Do not impose an arbitrary universal minimum. Prioritise evidence in these search bands:

- companies: approximately £500+/month, £1,000+ per engagement or clearly material recurring staff/agency expenditure;
- individuals/prosumers: approximately £250+ per completed outcome, £100+/month recurring, or a clearly evidenced high-frequency transaction stream;
- explicit premium tiers: a material price difference paid for speed, delegation or lower effort.

These are discovery anchors, not assumed viable prices. Lower spend can survive only with exceptional self-service distribution, repeat volume and support economics. Larger spend does not survive when expertise, risk transfer or physical work is the purchased core.

## 7. Promotion gate for a convenience candidate

Before DESK-VALIDATION or OUTREACH-READY, establish:

1. exact payer, budget owner and purchase trigger;
2. M2–M4 evidence of current spending;
3. what proportion of the purchase is genuinely convenience;
4. complete result a small product can deliver;
5. accessible inputs, D0–D4 documentation and verification oracle;
6. first-use time-to-value and total adoption burden;
7. recurrence or credible transactional volume;
8. direct and adjacent competitors, including the existing service;
9. both AI tests;
10. an exact public first-ten route;
11. realistic route to €5k–€10k+ MRR with labelled assumptions;
12. strongest second kill, especially why the payer will retain the human service.

OUTREACH-READY remains only permission to propose a bounded falsification message. BUILD-READY still requires direct buyer evidence and demonstrated delivery.

## 8. Convenience-specific kill questions

Ask explicitly:

- Is the customer paying for trust, reassurance, accountability or somebody to blame rather than task execution?
- Is the premium caused by scarce labour, physical access, equipment, licensing or relationships?
- Does the service provider absorb exceptions and emotional support that would become founder support?
- Is the purchase rare, bespoke or bundled with a larger transaction?
- Does software onboarding create more effort than it removes?
- Can the buyer use AI, a marketplace, an assistant or the incumbent to obtain the same convenience?
- Would a lower-priced product face consumer CAC, churn, refunds or support that destroys the economics?
- Is “wealthy buyer” merely a demographic assumption with no reachable channel or current purchase evidence?

A strong price is not a survivor if these questions fail.
