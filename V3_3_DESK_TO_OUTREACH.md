# Software Opportunity Hunt — V3.3 Desk-to-Outreach Boundary Amendment

**Approved:** 2026-10-12 by explicit user instruction  
**Status:** active authority; supplements V3, V3.1 and V3.2 without weakening BUILD-READY or commercial gates  
**Scope:** bounded discovery, OUTREACH-READY evidence, selection hypotheses and lower-price distribution economics

## Purpose

V3.2 attempts 401–500 established that paid sidecars and reachable categories exist, but the method still tended to demand public proof that a buyer would select a new entrant before allowing direct validation. That proof is normally private and is often the purpose of outreach.

V3.3 moves the unresolved selection question to a cheap, bounded outreach test when desk evidence is otherwise strong. It does not treat uncertainty as a pass, authorise contact automatically, or reduce the BUILD-READY threshold.

## 1. Bounded market-entry rounds

Do not default to another 100 attempts after a credible set of market-entry structures has appeared. Unless the user explicitly requests 100, use **15–20 materially distinct cases** and compare them deeply.

For the first V3.3 run use 20 new cases. Each must be a distinct buyer/job pair and must still satisfy honest counting. Competitor, access, AI and second-kill searches remain follow-ups, not attempts.

The run may promote no more than three propositions to OUTREACH-READY. This is a quality ceiling, not a target.

## 2. Selection hypothesis versus selection evidence

Record these separately:

- **Selection hypothesis:** an evidence-backed reason a defined buyer might choose or trial the entrant, such as substantially lower setup, self-serve completion, a neglected trigger, a segment excluded by incumbent economics, independent cross-system output, or materially faster first value.
- **Selection evidence:** direct buyer behaviour—reply, interview evidence, pilot, payment, commitment, switching or credible purchase intent.

Desk research may support a selection hypothesis. It normally cannot establish selection evidence. Never describe the hypothesis as observed willingness to pay.

## 3. Revised OUTREACH-READY boundary

A proposition may become OUTREACH-READY when all of the following are documented:

1. a real recurring or transactionally repeatable complete job;
2. meaningful bounded consequence, spend or paid coexistence;
3. an exact buyer and likely budget owner;
4. accessible inputs and a practical return path;
5. known direct/adjacent competitors and an explicit reason the proposal may still be selected;
6. acceptable founder-fit, liability, integration and support exposure for a small test;
7. both AI tests and a separate second-kill pass;
8. a public route to at least ten named prospects in the exact buyer class;
9. a precise, low-friction falsification question or offer;
10. all remaining unknowns labelled honestly.

The ten prospects do **not** need public proof of individual dissatisfaction before contact. They must genuinely match the buyer class and trigger context; a generic directory dump is insufficient.

OUTREACH-READY means “worth a bounded falsification message,” not “validated,” “recommended to build,” or “likely to buy.” Contact still requires the standing user-authorisation boundary.

## 4. BUILD-READY remains unchanged

BUILD-READY still requires direct customer evidence, preferably a paid pilot, deposit, commercially clear commitment or multiple credible willingness-to-pay signals, plus demonstrated deliverability and acceptable operating risk.

No amount of desk research, named prospects or attractive arithmetic confers BUILD-READY.

## 5. Lower-price products

Lower pricing may survive when all of the following are evidenced:

- self-service or genuinely low-touch acquisition;
- a broad reachable buyer population or marketplace/distribution route;
- fast first value and negligible migration;
- low marginal support and operating cost;
- credible recurrence or transaction volume.

Do not use a large theoretical population to excuse weak distribution, low retention or unsupported volume. Model arithmetic as a scenario, never observed demand.

## 6. V3.3 case selection

Prefer cases with observable paid coexistence, switching, independent outputs, public buyer populations and an entrant advantage that can be tested without a build. Avoid another sweep dominated by plugins whose integration burden is the product.

For each case capture:

- buyer/job and trigger;
- existing spend/workaround;
- principal system and complete outcome;
- adoption burden;
- proposed selection hypothesis;
- competitor grade;
- AI substitution;
- first-ten route;
- cheapest outreach falsification;
- strongest second kill;
- verdict.

## 7. What remains unchanged

All V3/V3.1/V3.2 controls remain unless this amendment explicitly changes the desk-to-outreach boundary:

- Ireland → UK → EU/EEA priority;
- default no-purchase assumption for ordinary feature improvements;
- complete sidecar outcome and adoption-friction test;
- evidence-supported economics and honest unknowns;
- founder-fit, liability, integration and support constraints;
- C0–C4 competitor grading;
- both AI tests;
- moat-narrowing prohibition;
- graveyard integrity;
- no outreach, spending or building without authorisation;
- zero survivors remains valid.

## Decision standard

V3.3 prevents the hunt from rejecting every credible entrant merely because willingness to switch cannot be proven publicly. It permits a small number of precise outreach hypotheses while keeping customer evidence as the boundary for build approval.
