## What the run learned

1. **Real spending is not enough.** The strongest money trails—EDI mapping, museum migration, crystallography, microscopy, takeoff and regulated authoring—purchase integration, specialist interpretation or accountable acceptance, not a standalone software file.
2. **Formal schemas are usually already productised.** Crossref, GenBank, ST.26, GTFS, SSIM, XLIFF, SPL, NACHA and EPCIS all looked deterministic. Official validators, incumbent platforms or required responsible-party inputs removed the entrant wedge.
3. **AI attacks labour but often leaves the non-software remainder.** P&ID extraction, music engraving, parts matching, takeoff and SDS drafting can all be accelerated. The residue is precisely the part the buyer pays to have verified.
4. **Prosumer convenience is easy to find and hard to monetize.** The 30 personal jobs were generally one-off, creative, physically verified or low-ticket. Faster generation increases substitution rather than pricing power.
5. **Deadlines often pay the workflow owner.** Rush value accrued to printers, regulated publishers, integrators, studios and permit specialists with the system or physical handoff—not to an independent converter.
