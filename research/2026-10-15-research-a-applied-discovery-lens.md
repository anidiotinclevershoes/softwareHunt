# Research A discovery lens — applied to the V3 hunt (15 October 2026)

**Status: non-authority research operating aid.** This document applies the already-committed Research A evidence to new desk research. It does **not** amend V3–V3.7, alter promotion gates, revive historical kills, require a marketplace model, or claim approval of a new method. Existing AGENTS/START_HERE authority takes precedence.

## Commercial intelligence, not a product template
Research A found commercially meaningful examples of (a) full digital jobs embedded in a paid principal system; (b) buying-intent interception at an alternative-search moment; (c) first client-specific job followed by repeatable channel discovery; and (d) a knowledgeable channel partner distributing a bounded result. Counterexamples showed that high MRR can still fail through retention/margins, and that marketplace listings/installations do not guarantee paying customers.

## Execute alongside current M/S/C/D evidence gates
1. **Identify the existing expenditure or buyer-side measurable loss first** using official fees, invoices, event exhibitor order forms, named user complaints, and real paid add-on listings. Separate price, buyer's actual payment, avoided losses, total project spend, and platform fees. The customer buying a larger human service does not validate the pricing of its software component.
2. **Name the buyer-controlled finished result, not the helper feature.** Examples: a buyer-accepted evidence pack, a completed ticket merge with writeback, an approved timesheet required to invoice, or an authorised licence cancellation. Ask how the customer verifies acceptance and whether accessible data permits reliable automated execution.
3. **Inspect the actual existing buying surface:** native marketplace listing, consultant/referral network, incumbent-alternative search, public purchasing form or qualified buyer directory. A platform channel is *not* free distribution. Distinguish discovery route from conversion/sales channel and assess reputation, reviews, vendor roadmaps, onboarding, permissions, support and security obligations.
4. **Grade separately:** M0–M4 economic evidence; S0–S4 independently capturable digital spend; C0–C4 incumbent proof; D0–D4 implementability/verification. Use the strongest original V3.4/V3.6 definitions, and never use an app installation, website traffic, user complaint or vendor testimonial to assert S4.
5. **First and second kill:** exact paid incumbents and their most complete outcome, official/native substitutes, customer AI plus files, incumbent AI-building, platform/API control, switching burden, manual expert exceptions, buyer access and economics. Competition can validate budget while independently killing entry.
6. **Promote only within existing V3.3:** real buyer/job, measurable material payment, plausible *independent* selection, complete output, acceptance oracle, vendor/AI/second-kill passes, and >=10 named matching prospects before OUTREACH-READY. BUILD-READY only with buyer commitments and demonstrated reliable delivery.
7. **Do not use this lens as an excuse to reroll the V3.7 frozen entries** (#961–1060); the 15–20 exploratory V3.3-style sidecar is independent of the numbered manifest unless the user explicitly commissions the frozen hundred-case run.

## Research A's new adversarial questions
- Did the first buyers come from a founder's personal account, previous agency clients, audience, capital or long-term reputation?
- Was the buying channel a platform privilege, not something a new entrant could reproduce?
- Does the expensive end-user outcome need an incumbent's permission, exclusive data or a licenced human signoff?
- Will the customer purchase an independent result *in addition* to its existing system, given exact alternatives?
- Do required support, data processing and integration obligations make five high-ticket subscriptions infeasible for a single founder with a full-time job?
