**Date:** 2026-10-15 (Ireland)
**Status:** Unnumbered exploratory research sidecar, **not** a V3 authority amendment, not a certified #961–1060 run, not a board promotion.

## User direction and exclusions
The user has asked to retain the lessons from the recently closed *Market Entry Gradient* conversational exploration, and then run a new money-first, acquisition-first search. Start with common and even apparently saturated markets, then test whether there is a commercially meaningful way into a sizeable market. **Do not** rescue a weak hypothesis by carving out a tiny niche between incumbents. Explore unusual markets and buying mechanisms without drifting into generic AI apps, mere reporting dashboards or reconciliation. Explicit exclusions: **photo management and family organisation**, including adjacent variants. This excludes the photo management hypothesis from this *new* search portfolio without rewriting its historical BANKED record.

## Retrospective context — conversation-level synthesis, not fresh verified market evidence
The conversational Market Entry Gradient explored 20 familiar parent markets and 40 speculative variations. It screened 24 later commercial propositions and ran a final desk gate on automated business-system implementation, which was not recommended for development. The counts are *conversation-level screening summaries*, **not certified distinct V3 opportunities, source audits, buyer interviews, or additions to the immutable graveyard**.

Key research learnings:
1. Established products often already deliver the *complete* outcome cheaply; functionality alone does not establish a sales reason.
2. Being able to capture a commission or participate in an existing transaction is not an automatic moat; marketplaces require demand, supply, trust and sometimes physical fulfilment/capital.
3. Selling cheaper versions of broad operating systems risks underestimating migrations, integrations and support.
4. Published vendor/service prices establish M2 offers at best, not independently purchased spend or entrant WTP.
5. Public award records, buyer-authored requests, ecosystem purchase triggers, membership/exhibitor directories and established paid acquisition channels can expose *reachable buyers and purchase moments*.
6. Distribution via an existing marketplace is a route to discovery, not proof of customer conversion.
7. Commercial headroom is important: an initial bounded outcome may be small, but a permanently tiny buyer pool or incidental feature fails.
8. Build viability needs a complete, independently valuable, repeatable, verified result and low-support delivery.

## Bounded search protocol (newly user-directed sidecar)
- **First find a public economic artefact:** buyer award, contract, paid commission, advertised acquisition cost, order, or buyer-side purchase statement; prefer Ireland > UK > EU/EEA.
- Record who pays, why now, purchase amount/rate, recurrence, current provider, accessible distribution, and why the outcome is independently purchasable (M0–M4 and S0–S4). Do **not** equate contract value, seller price or installation count with a buyer's willingness to purchase our SaaS.
- Test the *commercial purchasing structure*, not just a feature gap; require a plausible path to many buyers and first ten contacts at manageable cost.
- Run competitor C0–C4, documented implementation D0–D4, repeated-general-AI/AI-build, founder operation, buyer selection, support and second-kill checks before promotion.
- Use **15–20 materially different buyer + trigger + purchased-outcome cases** per bounded exploratory pass, with separate competitor follow-ups; label incomplete rows. Cross-check against prior research/legacy kills; no automatic reopening.
- After at most **two targeted evidence checks** of an economic unknown, mark it unproven and move on. **No forced survivors.**
- **Stopping rule:** at most six desk-deep candidates, three adversarial finalists, and one-to-three customer validation hypotheses only if all pre-outreach gates pass. No outreach, software build, purchases, merges or changes to main without separate user authorisation.
- The frozen V3.7 randomised #961–1060 manifest remains **UNRESEARCHED**; these are separate unnumbered explorations.

## Earlier cycle's conclusion
The final tested implementation-automation proposition was not supported as founder-operable independent software: simple migration is served by vendor/native low-cost tools; expensive full deployment requires business decisions, reconciliation, integration, verification and ongoing specialist/accountability work. This is a *hypothesis decision in a conversational search*, not a retroactive board kill.
