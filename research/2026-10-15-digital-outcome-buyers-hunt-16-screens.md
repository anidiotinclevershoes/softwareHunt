## Adversarial second pass on seemingly strongest four

### A — Managed technical SKU enrichment (R01)
- **Positive:** UK vendor advertises £535/mo paid complete structured records, and a Jan 2026 buyer-side public problem directly mentions spreadsheet/schema and disjoint imagery. This is the only strong new **recurring B2B complete-digital job** in the screened cohort.
- **Negative:** Buyer did not disclose actual supplier payment; vendor may deliver expensive exception-heavy expert QA and proprietary sourcing. Start with Data has buyer/client case studies (including established distributors), and builds SKULaunch; Plytix owns PIM record flows and offers AI enrichment. Brand catalogue files, images, taxonomy and ERP integration make each customer's acceptance different.
- **AI substitution:** General LLM can produce field mappings/description drafts, but must verify sourced specifications; a dedicated product would need repeat source-evidence trails, deterministic field validators and efficient handling of ambiguous attributes. **AI-build:** an entrant could replicate the first-pass extraction cheaply; competitor can copy it. The difficult part is accurate provenance and enterprise onboarding.
- **Verdict:** **EVIDENCE-INCOMPLETE**, not research-board promotion. If revisit, find 10 *named small* UK/Irish B2B distributors with the same single-category schema and evidence that at least two **currently pay an outside provider for acceptance-tested structured records**, rather than merely subscribing to PIM or accepting consulting.

### B — Book indexes (R02)
- **Positive:** £3.80/page recommended fee means about £1,140 for a 300-page project, a digitally deliverable file, time-boxed before final printing. Publishing professionals and authors can be identified.
- **Negative:** Indexer expertise is the purchased result; objective acceptance requires a knowledgeable human to judge omissions, hierarchy, subentries and crossreferences. New entrant competing with professional author trust/AI beta; low repeat per individual author. A publisher procurement contract would involve editorial proofing and slow trust-building.
- **AI substitution:** Automated index candidate generation is possible; cannot assume accepted production index unattended. **AI-build:** IndexPDF publicly advertises a whole-book AI beta; no defensible feature moat.
- **Verdict:** KILL autonomous SaaS; not OUTREACH-READY on the strength of a fee guideline.

### C — WCAG manual audit (R12)
- **Positive:** A genuinely paid digital PDF report often £449–£2,500+; public standards and WCAG rules enable objective test coverage for many checks; website owners are reachable.
- **Negative:** A paid manual audit is categorically different from a scan. UK GOV guidance says automation does not find every error. Keyboard, zoom, screen-reader and task completion involve direct manual judgment. 2026 vendor offers provide clear proof that this expertise is what the customer buys.
- **AI substitution:** Axe/Lighthouse and LLM generate partial issue lists cheaply; **AI-build:** low technical barrier to cloning a partial scanner means competition and trust issues. Neither equals full accessibility assurance.
- **Verdict:** KILL automated complete-service replacement absent a human-specialist review requirement (founder-fit kill).

### D — Trademark conflict watch (R09)
- **Positive:** Published independently charged, recurring UK trademark watch from £15/month and professional £600+VAT/year offers; weekly journal is public and predictable; source identifiers and dates allow deterministic monitoring.
- **Negative:** Exact low-price automated vendors already sell the job for £10–£20/mo; legal firms charge for risk judgement/representation. Monitoring is not equivalent to filing opposition or enforcing rights. High-ticket recurring wedge cannot be supported by source prices.
- **AI substitution:** Deterministic search + LLM similarity scoring can approximate common alerts; **AI-build:** easy for firms already controlling customer trademark portfolios. Credible first-ten users might exist but no reason to select unknown newcomer.
- **Verdict:** KILL generic monitoring; no right to narrow indefinitely to resurrect.

## Cross-case learnings
1. **Highest fees attach to human judgment and client assurance.** Indexing, accessibility, audio mastering and business-specific web changes are priced because software alone does not complete the accepted result.
2. **Lowest-friction digital deliverables are already highly commoditised** (image clipping, screen art separation, virtual staging, KDP interiors). They would require high volume and a defensible distribution advantage that was not found.
3. **Recurring B2B spending exists**, but the independent-money bridge is usually shallow or already controlled by established partners (PIM ecosystem, planograms, trademark, managed security). A new product's low cost of development gives incumbents the same ability to automate it.
4. **Direct customer language is useful but insufficient:** one buyer complaining of messy supplier catalogues is better than generic vendor copy, but it is not an observed third-party buying decision, let alone willingness to pay this founder.
