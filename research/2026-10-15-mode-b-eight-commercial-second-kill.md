## Cross-case adversarial evaluation

All eight were tested against the same founder-operability criteria. AI assistants can create plausible application screens; this is not evidence that they can repeatedly produce accepted payments, migrations, clinical records, bookings and rights-cleared outputs without specialist exception handling. D3/D4 verified fixtures and buyer-provided accepted work were not obtained. In the two PARK cases, general AI plus current inputs threatens customer-paid separation: human tastes/fit dominate styling, and riding-centre equine availability/consent demands safe venue operation. The prominent incumbent paid offers demonstrate an established market but are not proof of a reason to choose this new entrant.

**Result:** 8 screened; 6 proposed-entry REJECT; 2 exploratory PARK; **0 newly promoted DESK-VALIDATION, OUTREACH-READY or BUILD-READY**. The parent industries are not declared dead; the eight specific entrant theses do not clear the test.

**Most decisive next test:** find independently documented cases of at least three UK holiday-park operators *actually switching* full park-management suppliers within the last year, with buyer-described reason, actual total spend including migration/training, and time required. Test whether a new entrant can supply the *entire* purchased outcome at markedly better economics than ParkSphere, whose site already includes data migration, staff training and a published tariff. If not, close this entry instead of narrowing to a tiny feature wedge. A named venue roster is not a sales channel.
