## Evidence-backed anomaly investigations

1. **“Shopify cannot sync bundle stock.”** A specialist's marketing article said this broadly. [Official Shopify documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles/eligibility-and-considerations) says supported bundles use real-time component-based availability. Native limitations still exist, including location handling; do not inflate those into no native capability. Broad gap rejected.
2. **“No affordable indie licence service.”** The gap-directory article was not buyer research. Cryptlex's price was verified, but Keygen's free Dev tier (100 active licensed users/10 releases), offline licences and free self-hosting weaken the claimed empty market. No inference of cheap managed equivalent for every customer.
3. **“Pay for signing and warnings disappear.”** Some seller pages promised instant SmartScreen reputation. Microsoft's current documentation says reputation builds over time and EV no longer grants the old blanket bypass. Do not sell a guarantee the entrant cannot control.
4. **“New digital-waste rules require continuous online operation.”** The real October 2026 reporting trigger was checked against government guidance, which also describes retaining written outage records and submitting later. No independent evidence was found that the apparently exact offline newcomer has customer adoption.
5. **“The perishable shipping app is only $15.”** Full pricing separates advanced rules from integrations. ShipStation, subscription apps and carrier-transit groups can add $100 each. This increases observed spend but does not create an uncovered job. The vendor's savings calculator explicitly labels defaults illustrative; none became measured customer ROI.
6. **“PunchOut is necessarily a $15k+ gap.”** Enterprise pricing was checked on TradeCentric, then attacked with the $399/month Shopify offer and reviews. A real lower-priced tier exists, alongside custom-support requirements.
7. Several very exact new-site claims lacked independent adoption evidence. These remain C1 unless public documentation/pricing raises them to C2; neither C1 nor C2 means proven sales. No candidate was killed solely by a suspiciously well-matched landing page.

## Economics without invented demand
PunchOut has a credible few-customer revenue shape—13 customers at an observed $399/month would be $5,187/month—but that arithmetic is not an attainable pipeline or evidence we can support them. BI Helper's $50 starting price would require 100 customers to reach $5,000/month; GiftCard Hero's $19.99 would require 251 store subscriptions. EasyFixtures at £99/year needs about 607 annual buyers for a £5,000/month equivalent run rate. These same-currency scenarios only expose acquisition/operating demands; they are not forecasts or EUR conversions.

High service fees in research processing, migration, engineering and creative production cannot be transferred to software pricing without proving which valuable accepted work is automatable. Lower-price opportunities remain admissible under V3.3; no new £500/event minimum was imposed.
