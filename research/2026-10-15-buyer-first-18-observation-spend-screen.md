**Status:** completed bounded **unnumbered** exploratory screen; not numbered attempts #961–1060; no product promotion. **Geography:** Ireland and UK. **Authority:** V3–V3.7 unchanged. **Exclusions:** photo management, family organisation; no prior graveyard reopening.

## Scope, counting and source quality
- **18 different buyer + purchase trigger + purchased outcome rows**: 9 recorded public payments (A), 6 fee-bearing distribution/customer-acquisition categories (B), 3 contract/published-output observations (C). Some rows share suppliers/platforms but the buyer/job and payment decision differ. No claim of 18 completed V3 commercial validations.
- The UK's **Payments Transparency Register** allows a factual distinction between **actual payment** (M4) and an **awarded maximum** or **advertised seller price** (M2). Its recorded payment amount is not the supplier's margin, is not proof an adjacent task can be independently bought, and is not the entrant's attainable price. Source: https://payments-transparency-register.supplier-information.find-tender.service.gov.uk/
- For each row, **M** = strongest spend evidence (M0–M4); **S** = independent software-capture evidence (S0–S4). **C** = strongest checked competitor depth; **D** = implementation documentation for proposed hypothetical software outcome (often *not assessed* where economics already fail). Labels **directional kill** and **incomplete** are exploratory verdicts, *not* additions to the formal numbered KILLED board.
- Payment dates and publication dates are distinct. Framework **values are ceilings or estimated call-offs, not disbursements**, and supplier-advertised claims are not independently confirmed buyer invoices. Data may be revised.
- Broad initial primary-artefact searches plus targeted competitor checks; no named prospects screened for exact fit, interviews, trials, customers contacted, or software built.

## Cross-cutting competitor and AI second kills

1. **Research purchase (A01) → automated strategic reports:** Gartner sells analyst consultations and proprietary insight, while [Attest](https://www.askattest.com/pricing) already provides software-plus-audience research execution. **Competitor C2, technical D1** for suggested unattended comparable result. Repeat generic AI may summarise public sources but cannot replicate proprietary data/interviews/analyst responsibility. Kill.
2. **Media buying (A02) → autonomous campaign profit generator:** [Smartly](https://www.smartly.io/product-features/automated-ads) already advertises cross-channel media optimisation; procurement payment includes channel spend and agency expertise, so M4→S2 leap invalid. **C2; D1**. AI substitution also plausible for simpler campaign tasks. Kill.
3. **Wedding venue paid enquiries (B03) → enquiry-to-booking conversion:** [Bridebook](https://support.bridebook.com/en/what-is-venuepro) already bundles hot-lead prioritisation, auto follow-up and ROI; [Hostology](https://hostology.co.uk/) from £45/mo covers first-enquiry to event delivery; [VenueBot](https://venuebot.io/research) markets dedicated AI booking. **C2+, D1** for full revenue attribution, calendar/deposit/lead data. Generic AI can draft replies; reliable booking requires integrations and cooperation. Kill.
4. **Trades lead conversion (B01/B02) → independent AI receptionist/quotes:** [Checkatrade](https://join.checkatrade.com/pricing/) includes quote/job handling, communications and AI features. Paid lead budget buys audience access, not a generic automation feature. **C2, D1**. Kill.
5. **Large licence/support payments (A03/A08) → independent maintenance alternative:** legal resale rights, platform entitlements and vendor service-level commitments are not externally replicable. **C4 incumbent/ownership, D0**. Kill.
6. **Survey platform (C01) → survey form builder:** validated methods, panel access, permissions and longitudinal comparable data constitute the service, not merely questionnaire UI. Attest and public-health specialist operators provide alternatives. **C2, D1**. Kill.
7. **Ticket marketplace (B05/B06) → cheaper transaction gateway:** platforms combine demand, trust and settlement; fee saving does not create demand acquisition. **C4 on marketplace-owned demand/channel; D1** for independent substitute. Kill.

## Findings and next *decisive* action
**Most valuable discovery is the measurement method, not a product.** Official disclosed **actual payments** enable reliable money-first exploration and avoid falsely using advertised supplier fees as buyer spend. However large expenditures overwhelmingly contained proprietary entitlements, human judgement, data collection, a two-sided audience or physical deliverables. Across paid acquisition channels, suppliers already sell bookings, leads, conversion systems and settlement as a bundled product.

**Commercial decision:** No product hypothesis justifies promotion. Avoid another generic tender-alert, wedding-CRM, lead-qualifier, consultancy-report generator, or new ticket/quote marketplace. Existing proposal for public-contract intelligence collides with established tender/intelligence tools; official award/payment access is **a research source, not evidence of new product demand**.

**Cheapest next experiment, if more founder-led research is authorised:** Select **five small/private-sector independently paid digital service outputs** where buyer-side invoice/commission data is obtainable and operational result has an objective acceptance test. Require a *buyer-side* repeat purchase and a viable first-ten route **before** broadening. Do not use huge government awards as a proxy for SME customer selection. If that fails, stop this buyer-first cycle and review whether the founder is willing to enter a service-first model; do not invent narrow wedges.
