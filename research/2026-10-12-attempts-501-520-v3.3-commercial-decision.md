## Executive decision

2. **Accessible-EPUB repair plus evidence pack for small independent presses**

The other 17 cases are killed. No weak case was promoted merely to produce a survivor.

## Deep attack 1 — PowerPoint to tested SCORM

**Complete result:** customer supplies a finished deck and target LMS/version; receives a SCORM 1.2/2004 ZIP that passes SCORM Cloud and a named LMS test, plus a short exception report.

**Commercial evidence:** a live specialist page quotes from £150 for automated conversion and from £500 for enhanced conversion. iSpring prices its authoring suite at $970/year and an AI tier at $1,290/year. The economic hypothesis is therefore not “better PowerPoint”; it is pay once for a deployable package when annual authoring software is irrational.

**Competitors:** iSpring, Articulate/Captivate class authoring tools, the named specialist service and LMS vendors.

**Why a new entrant might be selected:** occasional converters can avoid a seat, learning curve and compatibility debugging. Narrow positioning would be fixed price, one course, named LMS, delivery only after a clean test.

**AI substitution test A — can general AI complete it?** AI can draft quiz questions, narration and summaries. It cannot by itself guarantee correct package manifests, completion rules and behaviour in the buyer’s LMS.

**AI substitution test B — can incumbents absorb it?** Yes. iSpring already adds AI and broad LMS compatibility; an LMS vendor can add conversion. This sharply limits moat and makes speed/distribution decisive.

**Separate second kill:** the exact one-off service already exists. This survives only because selection is testable among occasional providers, not because the market is empty.

**First-ten route:** the public Trainers List says it covers 6,786 active UK training companies and exposes profiles. Initial named set: N-ergy Group, Kinetic Youth, MBKB, Intelligencia Training, Estio Training, FTW Training, Acorn Training, Makers Academy, JGA and Capella Associates. These are access leads, not claims that each has the pain.

**Falsification question:** “In the last year, did you have a finished slide deck that needed to become a working SCORM package? What did you use, what did the final test/fix cost, and would a fixed-price one-off conversion for your named LMS replace that route?”

**Unknowns that remain:** frequency per small provider; how many already own an authoring seat; acceptable price and turnaround; how much redesign rather than conversion is normally required.

## Deep attack 2 — Accessible EPUB repair plus evidence pack

**Complete result:** customer supplies an existing EPUB and source assets; receives a repaired EPUB, Ace/EPUBCheck outputs, a manual-check record, truthful accessibility metadata and an exceptions list. The offer must not claim legal certification.

**Commercial evidence:** official EU guidance says EPUB validation is necessary and that automated tools cannot determine meaning or full usability. DAISY’s Ace is free and produces automated reports. AcePub advertises conversion from £0.50/page; specialist accessibility providers publicly sell manual remediation and reports. This proves a paid production job but also a low starting price.

**Competitors:** Ace by DAISY and EPUBCheck for free diagnosis; AcePub and accessibility/remediation studios for production; publisher production teams.

**Why a new entrant might be selected:** a small press with existing EPUBs may need a fixed-scope retrofit and evidence pack without buying a production system or commissioning a full reformat. The wedge is repair-and-return, not another publishing tool.

**AI substitution test A:** AI can propose alt text and code fixes. It cannot reliably determine contextual image meaning, reading order, mathematical semantics or real screen-reader usability.

**AI substitution test B:** incumbent conversion studios can add AI and already possess workflow expertise. The entrant therefore has no assumed moat; it must win on fixed scope, turnaround and transparent evidence.

**Separate second kill:** free diagnostics plus £0.50/page conversion may make the addressable pain too small. Outreach must establish actual failed-file frequency and whether evidence is purchased, not merely required in theory.

**First-ten route:** the Indie Press Network publishes named member presses and profile links. Initial set: 3TimesRebel Press, Afsana Press, Arachne Press, Arkbound, Asteria Press, Aurora Metro Books, Banshee Press, Barbican Press, Bitter Lemon Press and Black Cat Poetry Press.

**Falsification question:** “Has an existing EPUB failed an accessibility check, retailer request or reader test in the last 12 months? Who fixed it, what artefact did you receive, and would a fixed-price repaired file plus Ace/manual report be bought per title or batch?”

**Unknowns:** real failure incidence; exemption/territory effects; title volume; acceptable batch price; whether conversion vendors already include the evidence at no extra charge.

## Deep attack 3 — private/offline funeral tribute video

**Complete result:** a funeral director supplies a folder of family photos, optional clips and a licensed track choice; receives a correctly sized MP4 ready for the venue, with a fast local revision path.

**Commercial evidence:** official Obitus material shows a specialist team creates and quality-checks tributes. A UK crematorium publishes 2026–27 prices of £57 for a basic slideshow, £89 for a music slideshow and £118 for a themed tribute. Tukios claims 2.5m videos, 10,000+ funeral homes, a five-minute workflow, direct family uploads and AI-generated licensed music.

**Competitors:** Obitus/crematorium packages, Tukios/Tribute Technology, specialist funeral AV firms and generic slideshow editors.

**Why a new entrant might be selected:** independent directors may prefer local processing, no family-media upload, unlimited monthly output and edits up to the service. This is a selection hypothesis only; the run found no independent proof that UK buyers currently reject cloud workflows for privacy.

**AI substitution test A:** generic AI can assemble a slideshow, crop photos and generate music. It does not by itself resolve commercial music rights, venue playback constraints, tone, family approvals or deadline support.

**AI substitution test B:** Tukios already includes AI music and funeral-specific delivery. Existing funeral platforms can absorb the remaining automation quickly.

**Separate second kill:** this is the weakest survivor. Dominant venue/workflow coverage, emotional-support expectations and music licensing can erase the wedge. A negative ten-buyer test kills it immediately.

**First-ten route:** SAIFCharter publishes a table of independent funeral directors and towns. Initial set: J & S Funeral Service Ltd, Chris Davies Funeral Services Ltd, Richards Funeral Services Ltd, Mark Shaw Funeral Services Ltd, Ted Williams & Sons, D J Evans Funeral Director, Edward Carter Funeral Directors, Tonks Brothers Funeral Directors Ltd, Barton & Hallworth Funeral Services and Duncan & McCombie.

**Falsification question:** “Who currently turns family photos into the video played at the service? In the last ten funerals, how often did your team need a last-minute change or avoid uploading media, and would a branch-controlled offline workflow replace the current route?”

**Unknowns:** buyer versus crematorium control; existing Obitus coverage; music licensing cost; support burden; actual value of offline processing.
