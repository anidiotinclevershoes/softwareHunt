## Counting, boundaries and adverse checks
- 10 distinct buyer-authored purchasing occasions (A); 10 paid software/service spending categories with named buyers (B); 10 non-standard purchase/distribution mechanisms (D).
- **Exactly 30 observational rows**, and **not 30 new market opportunities**. Many deliberately fail; B/D include established solutions to test commercial headroom and alternatives. D10 checks same technographic lead job family as A01 and must not be counted as a new problem.
- A listing/budget is **M2**, not proof of award/payment. Upwork client lifetime spend and actual payment activity, if present, does not prove *this specific listing* was awarded or paid. Source dates reflect search result pages, which can contain relative-time errors; use explicit posted dates when available.
- B/D prices and fee structures generally **M2 offers**, not independent observed purchaser invoices. S grades refer to hypothetical new-entrant *separable complete outcome*, **not** the supplier's current income.
- A10 is buyer seeking UK/EU *data*, but client country unverified. A08 worldwide buyer with UK/EU targets. Keep local fit provisional, not UK customer proof.
- Active buyer submissions are time-sensitive; archived pages are **not an instruction to apply**. No marketplace outreach, procurement bid or vendor sign-up has been executed.
- "RESEARCH WATCH" is **not** the official banked/anomaly/outreach-ready status. Earlier historic KILLED, photo exclusion and CPO outreach unaffected.
- The design deliberately tests first-ten access and market breadth rather than demanding nonexistent competitor-free niches.

## Higher-depth second-kill comparisons (not counted as new cases)

### W1: Website provider footprint prospecting (A01, D10 plus independently observed corroborating listing)
- **Demand:** A01 UK agency budgeted $300 for operational repeat-use Windows/CSV PublicWWW script for Yell/Hibu website footprint × UK location combinations. **A second, distinct Upwork buyer** budgeted $300 for near-identical PublicWWW source-footprint and UK location search for *it'seeze/Spoton.net*: https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Build-Python-Tool-Identify-Business-Websites-Using-PublicWWW-API_~022110038686775173021/ . This second buyer is based in Serbia, not UK, and target site providers differ. It is **independent purchase intent evidence but not a 31st counted case**.
- **Interesting buyer-facing proposition:** verified, geographically filtered incumbent-site-provider lead lists with their found source footprints and refresh dates, not a generic lead-generation dashboard.
- **Incumbents:** https://www.wappalyzer.com/pricing/ (Pro $250/mo); https://builtwith.com/plans (Basic $295/mo, Pro $495/mo); https://builtwith.com/lead-generation (bulk export and filters); PublicWWW paid API must be funded, legal use and quotas checked.
- **Strongest kill:** A one-time buyer $300 script may be cheaper than recurring vendor subscription, but it proves precisely that customers may commission local utility scripts **instead of subscribing**. Paid data/API fees, copied-by-AI feasibility, licence constraints, accuracy/verification support, and credible first-ten matching web agency buyers unverified. Existing suppliers already deliver tech-qualified leads. **S1 only** for entrant. No outreach ready.
- **Next single falsification:** find three **independent** UK/IE web agencies paying repeatedly (not merely commissioning one-off script) for provider-specific, location-filtered *verified* lead sets; verify what they pay per month, data-source entitlement and why BuiltWith/Wappalyzer/manual searches are not acceptable. If unavailable after bounded checking, drop.

### W2: Conference-deadline animated promotion (A04)
- **Demand:** £/USD context: buyer listed $1,000 for time-critical 45-second brand video with stakeholder script and QR CTA; final sign-off and branded quality required.
- **Competitor:** https://help.vyond.com/hc/en-us/articles/17215758029460-Using-Vyond-Go-Text-to-video (2026 text/document/script to video workflow), https://www.vyond.com/plans/; freelance video market already offers finished work.
- **Strongest kill:** style, narrative and last-minute revisions are exactly the purchased value; licensed music/graphics and client approvals add human support. A general AI video product or human animator may adequately do the job; short deadlines are not proof of repeat software budget. **S1**, not a software candidate.
- **Next falsification:** independently paid accepted repeat same-format conference animation orders with fixed objective visual QA and a predictable recurring agency/channel buyer; otherwise kill.

### W3: Recurring workshops and memberships (B08, D02; related D06)
- **Spend:** ClassBento takes 20% for marketplace bookings but only 3.9% + £0.25 own-widget orders; Bookwhen Standard $29/mo already supplies memberships, class bundles, waiting lists and ticket handling. Sources above.
- **Potential economic hypothesis:** a seller with its *own* community might choose a cheaper direct checkout while using marketplace acquisition opportunistically. This is a well-known marketplace structure, not yet new evidence that customers pay a separate product.
- **Second kill:** the existing supplier can already offer precisely this (ClassBento widget, Bookwhen passes). Separate software adds attendee-data migration/booking support; demand share belongs to marketplace. **S1**, no independent benefit; do not rebrand as a micro-niche workshop pass calculator.
- **Decision:** reject generic booking, margin calculator, class memberships, or event loyalty as new products.

### W4: Brand-retailer collaborative sales (D04 / D05)
- **Money:** Faire 25% opening/15% reorders and net terms; Shopify Collective is free for eligible Shopify stores, with supplier fulfilled orders and synced inventory. Real user feedback at https://apps.shopify.com/collective-supplier (September 2026 supplier complains of retailers auto-added and vetting). This is **one reported product complaint**, not evidence of paid add-on selection.
- **Strongest kill:** paying for customer discovery, payment guarantee, credit risk and physical returns, not simple data exchange. Supplier vetting by itself is a *feature gap* which Shopify could add, and not an autonomous complete purchased job. **S1**, no new proposition.

### W5: Irish self-employed website purchasing (A02 / A03)
- **Evidence:** two distinct Irish buyers posted $600 WordPress sector site and $500 Wix redesign within days. These are direct **purchase requests**, not verified awards or monthly payments.
- **Strongest kill:** package-building sites/landing pages may be automated, but buyers want brand judgment, professional handover, SEO/portal functionality, revision and credibility; WordPress, Wix and AI site generators already address easy part. €/$500 one-off engagements may be service viable but don't establish a recurring standalone software buyer. No third-party separate product demand, **S1**.
- **Decision:** useful buyer acquisition lesson; not product recommendation.

## What this experiment changes about discovery
1. Buyer's *written requirements plus active budget and deadline* are far more discriminating than vendor rate cards.
2. Broad seller markets may be won through distribution/service speed, but that's distinct from a solo-operable independent software outcome. We should not force all buyer-authored jobs into SaaS.
3. Repeated **independent** buyer requests can justify a cheap commercial test even with strong competitors, but price sensitivity and one-off custom script supply are powerful negative signals.
4. The strongest new prospective job family found here (provider-specific web-agency sales prospecting) overlaps broad mature technographic incumbents; a tiny provider-specific filter is insufficient.
5. Incumbent price points remain useful calibration: low-ticket merchant systems are already inexpensive, while high-ticket managed services buy labour and accountability.
6. Source diversity remains imperfect: A dominated by freelance marketplaces, D by commerce platforms. **Do not claim this establishes the distribution of all opportunities across Ireland/UK**.

## Stop and next decision
**Close this bounded 30-observation experiment.** Do not inflate to 100 or endlessly narrow. No new OUTREACH-READY or BUILD-READY result. If the user wishes to investigate the only repeat-buyer anomaly, perform *one limited buyer-side evidence test* on website provider footprint prospecting (3 independent UK/IE agencies paying recurrently for verified supplier footprints, API economics, platform legality, a first-ten route). If that fails, shelve. Otherwise next research strategy should directly intercept purchase-trigger businesses and interview buyers, not generic vendor/pricing hunting.
