## Deep anomaly attacks

### 1. Vacant-commercial-property insurer inspection evidence — KILLED

- **Observed job:** some policy wordings require inside/outside inspection at least every 14 days with retained records; failing the condition may affect cover.
- **Buyer/access:** property managers and commercial landlords are readily identifiable; one public directory lists 1,504 UK property-management companies. This is contactability, not proof that they own enough vacant units or will buy.
- **Competition/spend:** Property Inspect publishes £45/£75/£245 monthly tiers; Inspection Manager £25/user/month; TouchRight starts at £10/month. Security firms also sell the completed inspection service and their own audit trail.
- **AI/substitution:** a scheduled mobile form with GPS/time/photo evidence is commodity functionality; generic inspection software or AI-video reporting supplies well over 80% of the proposed value.
- **Second kill:** the economically important outcome is a physical visit and escalation, not the database. A lightweight entrant either competes below an already low price ceiling or becomes a labour/security service.
- **Verdict:** directional kill; no outreach.

### 2. Private-water-supply operator compliance/action record — KILLED

- **Observed job:** commercial/public supplies including B&Bs, holiday lets and campsites are risk-assessed and sampled; frequency follows supply type, volume and risk.
- **Buyer/access:** identifiable rural hospitality operators exist, but the local authority is the regulator and normally owns the risk assessment/sampling event; consultants and accredited laboratories own technical work.
- **Competition/substitutes:** official/local-authority workflows, consultants, spreadsheets and emerging monitoring products already surround the operator. A useful extension quickly requires sensors, treatment expertise and alerts.
- **AI/substitution:** AI plus the regulator report can create an action list and log; software cannot replace certified sampling, analysis or treatment judgement.
- **Second kill:** the low-software portion is record keeping; the high-value portion is regulated technical service/hardware. No evidence supports £500–£1,000/month from small operators.
- **Verdict:** kill the standalone tracker; no outreach.

### 3. Forestry restocking-obligation tracker — KILLED

- **Observed job:** felling licences commonly contain restocking conditions; the Forestry Commission monitors them and can enforce. A 2026 case reported replanting plus ten years’ maintenance and £16,240 in fines/costs after non-compliance.
- **Buyer/access:** woodland owners/managers can be reached through RFS/CLA/Confor-type networks; that does not establish repeated software purchasing.
- **Competition/substitutes:** forestry managers already provide the service; broader forestry/tree systems exist; the authority retains the compliance relationship.
- **AI/substitution:** document extraction, calendar reminders and geotagged photographs cover most administrative value.
- **Second kill:** small owners face rare events and low willingness to pay; portfolio owners employ managers and broader systems. Owning correctness would create environmental/legal support risk.
- **Verdict:** kill as standalone SaaS; preserve only the general long-lived-obligation search pattern.

### 4. Licence-to-alter closeout/deposit release — KILLED

- **Observed job:** applicant package → surveyor review → legal licence → monitoring/final inspection → completion documents → deposit release. Transaction fees can be hundreds to several thousand pounds, but most are professional fees rather than software spend.
- **Buyer/access:** the TPI directory and public block-manager directories make many managing agents directly identifiable.
- **Competition:** Alter Portal already digitises the exact multi-party licence-to-alter workflow; adjacent block-management platforms and document-reading AI cover requests, dates and evidence.
- **AI/substitution:** AI can extract requirements, chase documents and compile the file. Surveyor/solicitor judgement, site inspection and consent remain the valuable core.
- **Second kill:** narrowing to only closeout/deposit release is a moat-narrowing rescue and a feature, not a company. Announced 2026 pressure to cap leaseholder permission fees adds buyer-margin risk.
- **Verdict:** kill; no outreach.

### 5. Football training-compensation recovery — KILLED for this founder fit

- **Observed job:** electronic player passport → allocation statement → compliance onboarding → request-to-pay → distribution. An Irish FA appeal demonstrates real calculation disputes and a substituted £10,500 award.
- **Buyer/access:** 128 UK professional academies and public county-FA/academy directories make organisations identifiable. This is a small specialist population, not a qualified buyer list.
