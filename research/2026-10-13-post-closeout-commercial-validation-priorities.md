## #761 — one gated technical test remains, but public fixtures do not prove the job

The original evidence still matters: an incumbent advertises roughly **£50–£80 per drawing** and describes repeated outsourced work across more than ten projects. The bought outcome is not generic PDF conversion: it is the customer's original DWG revised from construction markups, returned as correct editable DWG/PDF with revision control.

New counter-evidence:

- Autodesk's [Markup Import and Markup Assist](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/autocad/how-to-use-markup-features-for-easier-revisions-tuesday-tips-with-frank/) already overlay PDF/image markups and turn some detected text and MOVE/COPY/ERASE instructions into editable actions. A user still incorporates and checks them.
- Autodesk's [PDF import limitations](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/AutoCAD-MAC-Core/files/GUID-76FD0B6E-CE15-4EAC-B8EA-D83E4E1233EF.htm) say markups are not imported, compound objects are exploded, SHX text becomes geometry and precision is lost. A naïve conversion cannot establish correctness.
- [SketchCAD](https://sketchcad.co/) now markets browser conversion of drawings including “as-built markups” to DWG/DXF for **$2 for one export, $5 for 20 or $20 for 100**. It calls itself a drafting aid and requires professional checking. Its page does **not** prove semantic application of changes to the customer's existing DWG, preserved layers/title block or accepted revision history, so it is a serious substitute/price signal rather than an exact complete-outcome disproof.
- Public AutoCAD tutorials and published sample drawings are not a matched, permission-safe triple of **original DWG + actual markup + accepted revised DWG**. No such public commercial fixture set was located.

**Decision:** retain #761 only as an anomaly. Do not build a converter and do not use a synthetic demo as commercial validation. Run the technical test only if 10–20 permissioned completed-job triples can be obtained without paid acquisition or unsolicited outreach. Pre-register a demanding gate: at least **90%** of representative drawings completed without CAD-specialist correction, **zero unflagged material geometry/text/layer/title-block/revision errors**, safe refusal of every unsupported case, and an auditable diff. Test buyer selection only after that pass.

## #841 — post-run second-kill

The original £499–£1,499 service is still only **M2/S2 seller pricing**, not a buyer-observed repeated purchase. New evidence closes the standalone-product hypothesis:

- Autodesk documents the core [point-file-to-surface workflow](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Civil3D-Tutorials/files/GUID-E6432405-A737-489B-97DC-2555D21FF183.htm), including supplied point data and the next breakline step.
- UK [STC Suite](https://surveystocad.co.uk/) now claims CSV/coordinate-to-DWG/DXF, topology stitching, datum checks and custom layer templates from **£79/month**. It is explicitly a **public beta**; its [terms](https://surveystocad.co.uk/terms) are labelled a placeholder pending legal review, and no independent adoption evidence was found. This is competitor evidence, not S3 spend evidence.
- The Geospatial Survey Association says topographical surveys are [purpose-specific](https://www.gsa-uk.org.uk/getting-the-specification-right-for-your-topographical-survey-how-to-avoid-costly-survey-mistakes/) across detail, accuracy, grid/datum and deliverables; its [work samples](https://www.gsa-uk.org.uk/resources/work-samples/) are guidance and can be superseded by the project specification.
- An adjacent service, [SurveyFy](https://surveyfy.net/survey-data-processing/), explicitly combines automation with licensed surveyor review of every layer, QA and sealing. It corroborates that professional review remains part of the complete accepted outcome.

Even if the new beta product under-delivers, the combination of Civil 3D's documented workflow, low-cost direct productisation, existing bureaux and project-specific professional acceptance means a new standalone entrant has no evidenced capturable wedge. **#841 moves BANKED → KILLED.** Preserve its original manifest and completed-run count as history; this is a dated second-kill. Do not spend effort acquiring #841 fixtures.

## #825 — real service effort, no independent product demand

[The Photo Gatherer](https://www.thephotogatherer.com/digital-organising) advertises digital organising from **€1,000** and says a collection normally requires **20–40 hours**, covering multi-device gathering, culling, organisation and backup guidance. Its testimonials remain seller-hosted. Targeted searches found no independent invoice, paid marketplace review or buyer-side account establishing a ≥€750 digital-only purchase; more provider pages would remain M2/S2.

That evidence is consistent with the strongest counter-case: customers pay for someone else to take custody of a stressful one-off job, make subjective keep/delete decisions, navigate cloud exports and reassure them that nothing was lost. Existing photo software already performs much of the deterministic slice. **Keep #825 BANKED, but stop desk research.** Only three independently verifiable paid digital-only customers—and whether they would personally operate an offline tool below €250—can move it. That requires separate outreach permission or user-provided introductions/evidence.

## Commercial lesson

The 100-case run found genuine service prices but almost no capturable standalone-software spend: deterministic substeps are commonly cheap, native or already productised, while the remaining premium buys judgement, custody, liability, reassurance, revisions or physical/professional execution. The rational next evidence is therefore **closed-loop evidence already near the board**—live CPO replies and permissioned source→accepted-output pairs—not another speculative niche scan.
