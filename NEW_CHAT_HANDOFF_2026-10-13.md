## 7. Commercial reasons the last 100 failed (insight, not new authority)

The 100-case process did improve research quality. It did **not** produce a compelling software business. These recurring patterns explained the kills:
1. **Large public purchases are real spending but poor founder-access markets.** Example Oak Academy £66,666 bilingual captions awarded to the pre-integrated supplier, Hackney £43,873 1Spatial software already installed, FCA £90,427 data cleansing contract enterprise/procurement-heavy. Awards ≠ new entrant spending.
2. **Human operator owns complete outcome.** Engineers, translators, surveyors, editors, accredited professionals, creative directors, venue organisers and service teams get paid to handle ambiguity and take responsibility; automation alone produces a draft/partial sidecar.
3. **Many actual repeat digital transactions are low-priced.** Small marketplace/craft/artwork outputs, rush artwork, personal graphic design, £6/page DTP, £9 CAD floorplan uplift, less than €50 independent purchases. First-ten access alone cannot produce €5k MRR without incredible self-serve distribution.
4. **Every easy-to-verify digital part has native tools.** Autodesk/FARO/ODA, SCORM converters, InDesign scripts, Photo products, Canva, site-native CMS, Shopify/Amazon bulk import, free official checkers. One feature isn't a buying reason beside principal system.
5. **Express price deltas usually buy scarce *human* capacity or physical manufacturing**, not faster computing; price offer doesn't prove purchased rush demand.
6. **Recurring separable service is the right research direction**, but need actual buyer-side repeated paying evidence, fully verifiable outputs and a structural new entrant advantage. None established S4 or positive willingness-to-switch.

**Specific conclusion:** Do not assert "we're close" merely because spending signals became stronger. Commercial entry evidence is still poor. Founder cash and meaningful code are not justified by these cases today.
