# Software Opportunity Hunt — V3.2 Market-Entry / Sidecar Amendment

**Approved:** 2026-10-12 by explicit user instruction  
**Status:** active authority; supplements V3 and V3.1 without weakening the existing commercial gates  
**Scope:** competition interpretation, market-entry discovery, separable sidecar jobs, and adoption-friction testing

## Purpose

The first 400 audited attempts produced strong commercial kills but no new survivor. V3.1 correctly improved evidence quality, yet the combined method still risked treating near-whitespace as safer than entry into a proven market. V3.2 corrects that bias.

The new working hypothesis is that a viable founder-accessible business does **not** need an unpopulated niche. It may enter an established market where customers already spend, provided there is evidence for an underserved buyer/job and a credible reason to adopt. This is permission to investigate market entry, not permission to promote generic feature improvements or force survivors.

All existing founder-fit, evidence, price, AI, first-ten, outreach, build, counting, graveyard and second-kill gates remain active.

## 1. Competition is demand evidence, not automatic disqualification

Existing products may provide useful evidence that:

- the job is real and understood;
- a budget or transaction already exists;
- customers can be identified and reached;
- the market does not need to be educated from zero.

Competition does **not** prove an opening. Record which buyer, trigger and complete outcome each competitor serves; pricing; onboarding; switching cost; integrations; limitations; manual coexistence; and credible evidence of adoption. Kill when the same reachable buyer can already obtain the complete outcome at an acceptable price and friction. Do not kill merely because a broad category or incumbent feature exists.

Absence of competition remains ambiguous and receives no positive score by itself.

## 2. Default no-purchase assumption for feature improvements

Assume a customer will **not** buy another product merely because it performs one aspect of an existing workflow somewhat better.

A complaint, missing feature, awkward interface or incumbent weakness is insufficient. A candidate needs evidence that the value of the solved problem exceeds:

```
price + setup + learning + workflow fragmentation + switching/migration + perceived risk
```

A generic “better version of feature X” fails unless there is direct evidence that the target buyer already pays for an add-on, runs a costly workaround, changes supplier because of it, or has another observable purchase trigger.

## 3. Admissible sidecar job

Prefer a narrow recurring **sidecar job** that takes responsibility for a complete useful result while coexisting with the customer's principal system.

Seek evidence for all of the following:

1. **Defined trigger:** a document arrives, transaction changes state, failure occurs, request is made, deadline approaches, or another bounded event starts the job.
2. **Accessible input:** the buyer can provide the required data without prohibited scraping, privileged access, or an enterprise integration.
3. **Complete micro-outcome:** the first version completes a decision, transformation, detection, submission, recovery, booking, calculation or accepted deliverable—not merely organising work.
4. **Return path:** the result can be exported, handed off or written back into the existing workflow without forcing wholesale replacement.
5. **Observed workaround or spend:** paid add-on, specialist service, staff time, repeated manual process, parallel spreadsheet/system, or credible switching behaviour.
6. **Measurable advantage:** bounded time, money, risk, throughput or completed transactions sufficient to overcome adoption friction.
7. **Retention basis:** recurring triggers or credible transactional volume; not a one-off curiosity dressed as SaaS.
8. **Structural opening:** the incumbent is poorly positioned to own the result because it crosses systems, serves a neglected segment, depends on independent inputs, conflicts with incumbent incentives, or is too small/specialised for the incumbent.

Failure of one item may leave an anomaly. Promotion still requires every ordinary V3 gate.

## 4. Sidecar is not a loophole

Do not relabel a weak feature as a sidecar. Kill or downgrade when:

- the output is merely advice, a prettier view, another dashboard, generic drafting, reminders, storage or task tracking;
- adoption requires migration, enterprise security approval, sensitive credentials, many integrations or extensive configuration disproportionate to the value;
- the incumbent owns the authoritative private data or final decision and the product cannot produce a useful independent outcome;
- occasional ChatGPT/Copilot prompting supplies most of the value;
- the job requires continuing professional judgement, fieldwork or bespoke service;
- the only differentiation is a smaller ICP invented to evade an exact competitor;
- the incumbent can trivially absorb the complete job and there is no evidence customers will retain a separate product.

Initial concierge validation may test demand, but a permanently service-heavy operating model remains subject to the existing founder-fit and support limits.

## 5. Market-entry discovery lanes

For the next explicit 100-attempt V3.2 continuation, replace V3.1's default 60/20/20 mix with:

- **40 paid-coexistence cases:** paid add-ons, app/plugin marketplaces, specialist services, parallel systems or manual workarounds around established products;
- **30 system-boundary completions:** jobs that begin in one system or party and finish in another, with a bounded standalone output and practical return path;
- **30 underserved-entry cases:** established categories where a specific reachable buyer segment shows credible switching, exclusion, over-complexity, poor economics or an unserved trigger.

Every row must still represent a materially distinct buyer/job pair. Product terms may appear in an initial V3.2 query only when anchored to a named incumbent, integration, marketplace, workflow or observed coexistence artefact. Generic “software/app/SaaS/tool for X” searches remain barred. Competitor, access, AI and second-kill searches remain follow-ups and never inflate the attempt count.

## 6. Market-entry qualification

Before Product Workshop, record:

- established category and existing spend;
- exact underserved buyer and why it is meaningfully different;
- present principal system or supplier;
- trigger, accessible inputs, complete sidecar outcome and return path;
- evidence of paid coexistence, workaround or switching;
- adoption burden and time to first value;
- structural reason the incumbent does not already own the result;
- realistic price/transaction economics;
- named first-ten route;
- exact competitors and strongest reason no separate purchase occurs.

A proposition can survive with competitors. It cannot survive without an evidence-backed reason to be selected and retained.

## 7. What remains unchanged

- Ireland → UK → EU/EEA priority.
- Solo-founder build, operating burden and liability constraints.
- Evidence-supported economics; no invented willingness to pay, volume, TAM or success probability.
- Both AI-substitution tests.
- C0–C4 competitor grading.
- Separate first-kill and second-kill passes.
- Moat-narrowing prohibition.
- Graveyard integrity; prior kills require genuinely new structural evidence.
- OUTREACH-READY and BUILD-READY thresholds.
- Honest 100-distinct-attempt accounting.
- Zero survivors remains acceptable.

## Decision standard

V3.2 is sufficient to justify a materially different **market-entry** hunt while the other criteria remain intact. It is not evidence that survivors must exist. Its success condition is a fair opportunity for products that can win as low-friction complete sidecars in proven markets, without mistaking either competition or feature dissatisfaction for a business.
