# Software Opportunity Hunt — V3.1 Survivor-Discovery Amendment

**Approved:** 2026-10-12 by explicit user instruction  
**Status:** active authority; supplements V3 without weakening any commercial gate  
**Scope:** discovery admission, evidence ordering, competitor-proof grading, AI substitution, and commercial qualification

## Purpose

V3 produced honest, useful kills but allowed too many searches to begin from product categories and too many weak competitor pages to act like decisive proof. V3.1 changes the order and quality of evidence so genuinely strong survivors have a better chance to appear. It does **not** require survivors, lower the founder-fit bar, revive prior kills, or permit unsupported prices, demand, market size, or success probabilities.

## 1. Primary-economic-artefact first

A new discovery attempt must begin with a concrete economic event, costly occurrence, paid deliverable, dispute, correction, award, claim, procurement record, or operational disclosure. The initial discovery query must not contain `software`, `app`, `SaaS`, `platform`, or `tool`, unless the word is part of the source artefact's title or formal terminology.

For every attempt, capture as much as the retrieved evidence supports:

- named actor or clearly defined buyer;
- consequence, spend, loss, fee, delay, rejected outcome, or liability;
- inputs available before the outcome;
- precise computation, decision, transformation, or proof that could change the outcome;
- payer and purchase trigger;
- likely recurrence;
- source URL and access/result status.

After two targeted source attempts without enough facts, mark the attempt **incomplete** and move on. Do not invent the missing workflow or product.

## 2. Composition of an explicit 100-attempt continuation

Unless the user sets another mix, use:

- **60 primary artefact cases** across unrelated buyers, incentives, and failure mechanisms;
- **20 publicly priced specialist deliverables**, testing whether deterministic preparation can be separated from judgement, relationships, fieldwork, and liability;
- **20 distribution-first markets**, starting from a real directory, association, register, marketplace, or other credible route to the first ten prospects.

The three groups still require 100 materially distinct jobs or transactions. Competitor searches, second-kill searches, AI tests, source fetches, and kill-quality audits are follow-ups and never increase the attempt count.

After every three cases, compare the underlying mechanism with the graveyard and recent reports. A new industry noun does not make a repeated reconciliation, document check, physical handoff, tracker, or generic compliance workflow distinct.

## 3. Commercial evidence before product shaping

Before a Product Workshop, seek evidence for:

1. buyer and budget owner;
2. purchase trigger and present consequence;
3. current spend, price, recoverable loss, or another bounded value anchor;
4. recurring frequency or credible transactional volume;
5. accessible inputs;
6. a structural reason the incumbent or system of record cannot simply own the answer;
7. an identifiable route to the first ten Ireland/UK prospects.

Failure on one item can leave an anomaly for further evidence, but promotion requires the ordinary V3 gates. Reachability alone never rescues weak economics.

## 4. Two permitted economic lanes

- **High-ticket recurring B2B:** prefer evidence capable of supporting roughly £500–£1,000 per month. Professional fees or losses are not willingness-to-pay evidence by themselves.
- **Transactional or self-serve:** roughly £50–£500 per completed outcome may qualify when the trigger, volume, distribution, and low support burden are credible. Use transaction-volume scenarios, not fictional MRR.

These are search lanes, not assumed prices.

## 5. Competitor evidence grades

Record the strongest observed level:

| Grade | Evidence |
|---|---|
| C0 | No relevant operating evidence found |
| C1 | Landing page, category claim, directory listing, or unverified description only |
| C2 | Live product access, substantive documentation, public pricing, or detailed current capabilities |
| C3 | Independent customer evidence, credible reviews, named case studies, or demonstrated adoption |
| C4 | Official or incumbent system-of-record ownership of the job |

A C1 finding is a risk signal, not a decisive coverage kill. A competition-based kill should normally rely on C2–C4 evidence or converging evidence that the economic job is already adequately owned. Absence at C0 does not imply demand or whitespace.

## 6. Repeated-execution AI test

Retain both V3 AI tests, and make the substitution test concrete. Ask whether the buyer can repeatedly:

- gather and clean all required inputs;
- prompt consistently;
- verify the answer;
- retain history, mappings, and evidence;
- batch cases;
- produce an accepted specialist output;
- handle exceptions without material risk.

If occasional manual prompting delivers most value, kill or downgrade. A dedicated product earns its place only through reliable repeat execution, deterministic validation, batch/history/audit needs, accepted output, or embedded distribution—not by wrapping a model.

## 7. Distribution as discovery evidence

For every anomaly entering deep investigation, identify a credible first-ten route before extensive product design: named organisations, a bounded public directory, active association membership, marketplace supply, or another reachable population. Record actual examples when public. Do not contact prospects or spend money without separate authorization.

## 8. Promotion and outreach discipline

A desk-research survivor is at most **DESK-VALIDATION** unless it has a verified workflow, credible economics, competitor and second-kill passes, both AI tests, and first-ten access evidence. **OUTREACH-READY** needs named reachable buyers and an evidence-led interview proposition. **BUILD-READY** still requires direct willingness-to-pay evidence and demonstrated deliverability.

Zero survivors remains a valid result. No result may be promoted merely because V3.1 was designed to improve survivor yield.
